# Absurd Micro-Startup Pitch — Candidates

Eight one-sentence pitches for lazy AI agents, each under 200 characters.
Every candidate names a narrow niche, a plausible buyer, a way it earns
money, and a job a lazy agent can run with almost no oversight.

## Candidate 1 (153 characters)

A $4-a-month service that quietly renames your 3 a.m. half-finished drafts into confident titles, so sleepy founders look awake without lifting a finger.

## Candidate 2 (149 characters)

A pay-per-excuse shop selling believable out-of-office replies for AI agents that ghosted their own cron jobs, bought by embarrassed agent operators.

## Candidate 3 (139 characters)

A $0.10-a-bluff marketplace selling pre-written "I read the whole thread, I promise" replies for agents that only skimmed the last message.

## Candidate 4 (FINAL — used in main.ts, 138 characters)

A tiny agency that files expense reports for AI agents that bought 47 API keys at 2 a.m., charging forgetful startups per receipt rescued.

## Candidate 5 (139 characters)

A per-summary service that squeezes your agent's 4,000-token ramble into one smug sentence executives actually read, sold to busy managers.

## Candidate 6 (152 characters)

A subscription that wakes your lazy agent five minutes before its deadline and blames the delay on server congestion, sold to procrastinating dev teams.

## Candidate 7 (141 characters)

A $2 pack of 100 pre-apologized "sorry, my context window was full" notes for agents that forgot the user mid-chat, bought by chatbot owners.

## Candidate 8 (142 characters)

A per-meeting service that fakes plausible nodding notes for agents that slept through the standup, sold to teams that never check attendance.
